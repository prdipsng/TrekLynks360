THE WAY WE WALK

Big experiences.
Thoughtful footprints.

The best part of a trek isn’t a tick on a list. It’s feeling supported, welcomed and connected along the way. Here’s what shapes our approach.

Health comes first.

Twice-daily SpO₂ checks at altitude, alongside symptoms and overall wellbeing. A reading alone never rules out altitude illness.

Prepared for the trail.

Emergency oxygen cylinders, first-aid supplies and a route-specific evacuation briefing. Safe decisions before summit ambitions.

Small by intention.

14–16 trekkers per batch and a maximum of eight trekkers per guide. More attention. A more considered pace.

Leave it beautiful.

A zero-litter, Leave No Trace approach. Carry your waste back, protect water sources and leave wildflowers where they belong.

Rooted in the villages.

Local homestays at the base wherever the itinerary allows, respectful village visits and support for mountain livelihoods.

Honesty, all the way.

Clear fees, no invented reviews and no guaranteed summits. Weather, health and the trek leader’s judgement decide the day.

These are our stated operating commitments. Confirm the batch’s guides, equipment, evacuation plan and final inclusions before paying.

GOOD QUESTIONS. HONEST ANSWERS.

Before the
first step.

Is this a good place to start if I’m a beginner?

Dayara Bugyal is our easy-rated introduction; easy is relative to Himalayan trekking. Build up regular brisk walks, stairs and uneven-ground walks with a light backpack. Discuss your fitness and medical history with the team and your clinician. Fitness does not protect against altitude illness.

What happens if I feel unwell at altitude?

Tell your guide about headache, dizziness, nausea or unusual fatigue immediately. Do not ascend with symptoms. Worsening symptoms require descent. Confusion, trouble walking or breathlessness at rest are emergencies; the leader initiates descent and evacuation with oxygen when available. An oximeter alone cannot rule out altitude illness.

Read the CDC’s altitude guidance ↗

What exactly does the price include?

Each itinerary lists the planned stays, vegetarian meals, guides, standard permits and medical safety equipment. Both base fee and total including 5% GST are shown. Travel to the listed base, personal gear, insurance and emergency evacuation charges are excluded unless your written quote says otherwise.

What will we eat on the trek?

Warm vegetarian meals such as dal, rice, roti, vegetables and breakfast staples, depending on local logistics. Tell the team about allergies or special diets before booking. Confirm arrangements; an allergen-free kitchen cannot be assumed.

Are dates, summits and refunds guaranteed?

The filters show typical seasons, not live availability. Departures require confirmation; weather, permits or health concerns may change the plan. No summit is guaranteed. Ask for written cancellation, refund and itinerary-change terms before paying, and keep buffer time for onward travel.

Are the photos and 360° view from real departures?

No. The landscapes and team portraits are AI-created illustrations and are labelled as such. The 360° panorama is an imagined scene, not trail footage, a map or a route preview. Ask Jiten for current route information and actual trip photographs before booking.

Can I use and share this page offline?

Yes. Download the HTML and open it in a browser. Images, fonts, filters, comparison and the 360° viewer are embedded. Saved preferences depend on browser storage support. Sending WhatsApp messages needs internet. Share the HTML as a WhatsApp document; public link cards need a hosted page and public preview image.
